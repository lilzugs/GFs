## The rendering thesis

**Simplicity is a designed visual hierarchy, not merely the absence of detail.**

The intended read is mature, playful, affectionate, expressive, and hand-drawn: polished enough to be deliberate, loose enough to have personality. Avoid over-uniform vector-mascot polish, hyperrealistic surface rendering, glossy inflatable highlights, and mechanically perfect mirrored curves.

### 1. Ink hierarchy and line quality

- Use **very dark burgundy, plum, or near-black wine ink**, rather than requiring absolute black everywhere. Choose hue/contrast to suit the subject; do not recolor character-specific anatomy.
- **Tier 1—outer silhouette:** the boldest, most decisive contour around the character and large separate forms.
- **Tier 2—structural separation:** medium-weight lines for hair-over-face, ears, garment edges, limbs, expression-defining features.
- **Tier 3—interior marks:** fine, sparse, often tapering or broken indications for fabric tension, form changes, small creases and facial accents.
- Shape the stroke: taper endpoints; vary thickness around turns; let some interior marks vanish into flat color instead of boxing in every shadow.
- Avoid uniform-thickness tracing, redundant nested contours, doubled edges, and ghosted old silhouettes.
- Keep the strongest facial ink where needed for readability; *don't thin away the fixed expression*.

### 2. Color and shading

- Aim for one flat local color, one decisive shadow shape, and only occasional selective highlight for each major form. This is a **heuristic**, not an absolute numerical limit.
- Build form with large, contiguous, hard-edged shadow masses; do not scatter numerous weak micro-gradients or glossy spots across the surface.
- Let one shadow explain a volume; allow adjacent forms to have different amounts of shading.
- Choose highlights sparingly to support silhouette and focus. Do not create identical circular white gleams on every convex area.
- Even when the medium includes limited soft tone transitions, the **graphic color block** should remain the governing visual element.
- Keep **skin/fur, garment, hair, ears and hardware** unmistakably separate materials. Garment shadows must not look like skin shading.

### 3. Shape language

- Keep clean, easily read silhouettes, but avoid making every curve a perfect circle, oval, or mirror of its opposite.
- Small deliberate tangencies, slight line-direction changes, and subtle asymmetries give drawing energy.
- Exaggeration must be authored by the character's locked shape and anatomy, **not invented by a style transfer**.
- Maintain mature-adult proportions and species-specific facial identities; do not make every Growlfriend share the same generic humanlike face.
- Preserve each character's emotional signature. Most default expressions should feel inviting, sweet, affectionate, mischievous, or gently playful as appropriate to that character—not automatically confrontational or sultry.

### 4. Fabric and material behavior

- Use **very few carefully placed fabric folds**, generally where fabric pulls between anchors, meets a seam, or carries weight. One clear transverse or curved fold can outperform five overlapping wrinkles.
- Keep contours for **clothing** separate from anatomical outlines. Do not turn shirt shading into exposed-skin color or accidental cutouts.
- Preserve approved collar and hardware construction; gold receives small clean highlights and a dark edge, not heavy photorealistic metal texture.
- For bust, waist, and posture decisions, consult the **anatomy guide**. Rendering may clarify already-approved weight using light and cloth tension, but may **not** enlarge or reshape anatomy without permission.

### 5. Faces and eyes

- Prioritize legibility at thumbnail size; eye direction, upper lids, brow and cheek relationships carry character.
- Use simple graphic irises/pupils and restrained catchlights **only where compatible with that character's locked eye design**.
- Preserve facial locks exactly. Mallow's slim pink iris ring, black central pupil and single tiny white dot per eye are locked; do not add hearts, gradients, enlarged sclera, new lashes, or change her smile.

### 6. Hair, ears, and layer separation

- Define hair with a confident external shape and **one broad deliberate shadow** per meaningful cluster; minimize extra strand lines.
- Keep hair/ear/neck/shoulder overlaps unambiguous, with one readable boundary per layer. No double-lined or ghosted shoulder regions.
- Lop ears must keep a broad, visibly **pink inner ear** against a distinct outer fur surface. Avoid thin tube-ear silhouettes.
- When colored ears blend into a pink background, prefer a neutral **warm-white or lightly textured paper** background rather than collapsing the ear geometry.
- The paper texture should be subtle enough not to compete with flat coloring or look like skin texture.

### 7. Composition and animation studies

- For a neutral reference portrait, frontal symmetry and stable framing are valid; do **not** force a dynamic pose just to imitate third-party action illustrations.
- For later expression or animation tests, conserve main construction and move smaller elements—eyes, lids, brows, mouth, ear tips, hair ends—intentionally. Don't add constant motion everywhere.
- Static model-sheet clarity and expressive illustration are separate deliverables. Each can use the same rendering language without identical posing.

### Known past failure modes

- Long horizontal stacks of fabric wrinkles; duplicate underbust and shoulder contours.
- Glossy near-identical white ovals producing an inflatable or toy-plastic appearance.
- Rounded upper contours with too-uniform fullness; *address geometry only in an authorized anatomy pass*.
- Cream shirt merging visually into the lighter fur or skin.
- Pink ear interiors disappearing into a pink background, leaving tube-like ears.
- Hair/shoulder ghosting and ear-root/head “lumps.”
- Overly regular line weights and mirrored shapes; vector mascot effect.
- Structural drift when a prompt nominally requests ink or shading alone.
- Asking for “several passes” caused actual **multi-panel diagrams/comparison sheets**, not a refined single image. Do not use progress-chart language in production image prompts.

## Practical production workflow

**Before rendering**
1. Load the **exact approved character image** as the edit source. Read the relevant face/anatomy/costume locks and palette file.
2. Identify whether this is a **style** task, **surface cleanup**, or **anatomical redesign**. Do not mix them casually.
3. Name **one** local objective, plus exactly what must remain unchanged.

**For a rendering-style pass**
1. Preserve silhouette, geometry, pose, identity, crop, clothing construction, and palette.
2. Modify ink hierarchy and graphic shading *without* adjusting underlying anatomy.
3. Produce **one portrait**, not a grid, annotated sheet, numbered progression, arrows, or text.
4. Compare with the actual source: eyes, teeth, face, ears, collar, neckline, arms, bust/waist, colors, and framing. Reject attractive results that violate locks.
5. Only promote a new checkpoint when the user explicitly approves it.

**After rendering**
- Ask: “Is the first read recognizable at thumbnail size? Are the outer lines stronger than interior marks? Can three material classes be distinguished at a glance? Are any highlights or creases redundant? Did any anatomy or costume silently change?”
- Preserve earlier checkpoints and record both improvements and errors. A new render never retroactively overwrites previous locks.

## Reusable short prompt fragment

> Refine the supplied approved Growlfriends illustration **as a rendering-only edit**. Preserve the character's exact locked face, anatomy, silhouette, pose, outfit construction, palette, and framing. Use expressive dark wine-colored ink with strong silhouette contours, selectively tapered interior lines, large decisive cel-shaded shadow shapes, sparse carefully chosen highlights, and restrained physically sensible garment creases. Keep hair, ears, skin/fur, clothing, and gold hardware clearly distinguishable. Use an uncluttered warm-white lightly paper-textured background when colored shapes would otherwise bleed into the backdrop. Produce **one** clean finished illustration, **no captions, diagrams, multi-panels, or text**. No redesign.
