## 1. Authority and scope

This is the final right-facing rear-oblique visual master of the adult neutral clay mannequin Sandy. It supplements, not replaces, the independently approved frontal, front-oblique and 90-degree profile references. The goal is one consistent figure in rotation, not four attractive but incompatible figures.

The final image locks body shape, relative proportions, upright posture, relaxed arms, head angle, camera/framing and geometry-defining shading. It has a pink/lavender temporary palette; **that hue is NOT canonical**. Palette correction is a separate non-geometrical operation. Never let a recoloring pass redefine the posterior or lower garments.

The 2D images are visual authorities, not a measured mesh. Angles, volume transfers and apparent mass are qualitative art direction unless specifically measured from a future 3D model.

## 2. The posterior shape specification

- **Scale and envelope:** compact relative to the complete body; preserve approved total apparent size, lateral breadth, maximum rear projection, hip width and existing relationship to waist and legs. Not an enlargement request.
- **Distribution:** lower-biased fullness. The upper region is quieter and less uniformly rounded; the middle region transitions into a fuller lower form. The lower contour carries visual weight while the upper attachment remains in place.
- **Upper transition:** continuous slope from low back/hips into the main form. Avoid a sudden shelf, abrupt spherical start, or exaggerated hollow. Do not redraw the waist in order to make the posterior appear larger.
- **Lower contour:** rounded, broad and substantial, with a smooth continuous arc and a distinct transition to the slimmer posterior thigh. Avoid a high centered sphere, elongated sagging form, or sharply angular overhang.
- **Lateral transition:** follow the same attached volume into the side hip. No abrupt widening of pelvis or lateral thigh. Do not interpret posterior depth as X-axis hip width.
- **Central separation:** a single coherent separation line with believable occlusion and perspective. Do not invent extra seams or repeat historical contours. The near and far forms are two views of the same anatomy, not independently sized objects.
- **Thighs:** preserve slender upper legs and the small visible negative-space opening at their upper medial meeting point in the approved rear-oblique image. The opening is a product of contour and perspective, not an instruction to change stance, spread legs or hollow out the pelvis. Inner thigh edges taper naturally; knee placement and lower leg silhouette stay fixed.
- **Posterior thigh junction:** the hamstring region recedes beneath the lower contour, in agreement with the approved side profile `73361fbb-7bec-4deb-a048-c44ce75813e3`. A discernible crease should blend out laterally, rather than becoming a second outline or a clothing hem.
- **Anterior thigh:** the near arm largely occludes its anterior contour in this view. Do not restore the stray anterior thigh sliver seen in earlier iterations by broadening the leg in depth.

## 3. Sculpting vocabulary: volume lock

Use **volume-conserving redistribution** (constant-volume sculpting), not density increase, when the intention is to change where fullness resides.

| Term | Meaning in this workflow |
| --- | --- |
| Scale | Overall visual size of the feature |
| Volume | Total material represented by the feature |
| Distribution | Location of material within the feature |
| Projection | Extent in a given direction |
| Upper/lower pole | Upper/lower portions of the existing form |
| Gravity settling | Lower-biased apparent distribution without translating the whole feature |
| Rendering reset | Reconstructing surface tones/linework without changing geometry |

**Volume lock → upper-to-lower transfer:** treat the material like a fixed quantity of malleable clay. Offset a reduction of upper fullness with a corresponding lower-region increase. Preserve scale, maximum rearward projection, hip breadth, attachments, stance and neighboring leg geometry. Do not enlarge the entire feature, lower the complete pelvis, or raise the knees. Exact conservation cannot be enforced from independent generated 2D views; compare visual shape and all locked landmarks rather than claiming measured equality.

The same general principle applies to the independently approved bust: quieter upper convexity and weight toward the lower region, without moving the whole attachment or increasing total size. The actual bust silhouette is independently locked.

## 4. Surface-rendering rules and the ghost-contour failure

Several intermediate renderings retained old crescent-shaped shadows after the underlying shape had changed. This made the surface look like multiple overlapping shapes, seams or smudged outlines. A further geometry edit is NOT the remedy.

**Geometry locked; surface rendering rebuilt** is the remedy used immediately before the final approval.

1. Use a clean, unmarked source as the actual image-edit base. User markup is a diagnostic overlay only; never blend it into the output.
2. Remove redundant shadow crescents, bands of highlights, smudges, and obsolete internal curves in the affected area.
3. Reconstruct the continuous surface from the approved external silhouette inward. Do not trace or shift obsolete shadow boundaries.
4. Re-establish one primary contour and one coherent soft shadow transition per form, with a single appropriate contact crease at the leg junction.
5. Keep highlights consistent with the existing studio key light. Prefer broad readable gradients plus restrained cel-shadow shapes over glossy repeated edge ribbons.
6. Retain only believable fabric tension at the upper hip/waist and the junction with the thigh. Avoid concentric fabric ridges, piping-like outlines, artificial seams, or wrinkling that changes perceived shape.
7. Inspect both forms at normal viewing size and close up: neither should appear to have a smaller old version nested inside it.

The final master demonstrates the desired cleanup: the lower forms read continuously, the far/near edge relationship is clear, and the small upper-thigh opening is legible. This is **surface-rendering approval**, not a license to further alter the anatomy.

## 5. Permanent palette and garment construction

| Material | Canonical base swatch |
| --- | --- |
| Neutral grey sculpting clay (exposed head, neck, arms, lower legs) | `#606060` |
| Opaque white shirt and fitted knee-length bottoms | `#FFFFFF` |
| Black/near-black choker, cuffs, underbust band, knee bands | `#111111` |
| Collar hardware | Existing warm metallic gold (no new exact hex specified) |

These three hex samples were explicitly selected after sequential darkening of the clay swatch. They are base material references, not a requirement that every lit/shaded pixel equal the hex. Neutral grey must remain visibly distinct from white clothing; no lavender, peach, tan or frosting-like blending of the two materials.

The white bottoms run continuously from below the black underbust band to the **upper edge of the black knee bands**. Everything below the bands is grey clay, not white fabric. A false shorts hem at the glute-to-thigh transition is incorrect. Maintain sleeve cuffs, collar and gold O-ring. Do not include the temporary swatch card in production reference images.
